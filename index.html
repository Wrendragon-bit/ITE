OceanEye

Underwater species detection with a state-space YOLO model, built for the 2026 World Vocational College Skills Competition in Phnom Penh, 20 to 22 September 2026.

The problem

Find and name four seabed species in underwater images, video and live streams: sea cucumbers, sea urchins, starfish and scallops. Offline, on a single GPU.

Approach

A 72.4M-parameter YOLOv8 detector with Mamba state-space (VSSM) blocks in its backbone, trained on the URPC2021 underwater dataset at 640 × 640. The state-space layers run on a parallel scan written in plain PyTorch, so the model needs no compiled CUDA extension.

Results

mAP@0.5 of 0.867 and mAP@0.5:0.95 of 0.522 on validation, at 0.821 precision and 0.817 recall. 1.13 s per image, half the time of a step-by-step scan. Live video streams at about 26 fps while the boxes refresh about once a second. It knows only these four species, and accuracy drops on footage unlike its training data.

Stack

Python, PyTorch with CUDA, Ultralytics YOLOv8 and Mamba state-space blocks, with a Flask dashboard for images, video and live monitoring.

Team

Eng Dararoth and PovChomreoun Yuleang.

E-commerce platform

A multi-vendor Django shop: coursework for Year 2, Semester 2, built on Destiny Franks’ Django e-commerce course.

Starting point

The course’s store: catalog, search and filters, cart, wishlist, reviews, coupons, and separate dashboards for customers and vendors.

What we added

Checkout by KHQR from any Cambodian banking app, or cash on delivery, with the seller confirming each payment before an order counts as paid. Plus a blog and a shop assistant chatbot.

The chatbot

No language model. It scores intent from keywords and phrases, fuzzy-matches products, categories and vendors in the database, and answers from live prices and stock, so a product added a minute ago can be asked about straight away.

Stack

Python, Django 4.2, SQLite, HTML, SCSS and JavaScript.

Team

Eng Dararoth and Oul Borypitou.
